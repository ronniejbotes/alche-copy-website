**SEO takes three to six months to show measurable movement and six to twelve months to produce reliable lead flow.** That is the honest answer, and it is the one that loses agencies deals — which is exactly why so few of them give it.

The longer answer is more useful, because "six to twelve months" is not a waiting room. Specific things happen in specific months, and most of them are observable long before your phone starts ringing. If you know what to look for, you can tell whether SEO is working by month two instead of finding out at month nine that it was not.

Google's own former spokesperson Maile Ohye put it plainly: in most cases, SEO takes "four months to a year" to implement improvements and then see benefit. Nothing in the last decade has made that faster. AI search has, if anything, added a second scoreboard to compete on.

## Why SEO Is Slow (And Why That Is the Point)

Paid ads buy attention. SEO earns it, and earning takes longer than buying.

When you turn on Google Ads, you are renting a position at the top of the page. The moment you stop paying, the position disappears. When you rank organically, you are being vouched for by an algorithm that has to first find your pages, understand what they are about, decide whether you are credible enough to show, and then watch how people behave when it does show you.

That sequence has four separate delays baked into it:

1. **Crawl delay** — Google has to revisit your site and find what changed. On a small, low-authority site this can take days to weeks per page.
2. **Index and evaluation delay** — being indexed is not the same as being ranked. Google places new pages provisionally and adjusts.
3. **Authority delay** — links, citations and brand mentions accumulate slowly, and they are the strongest signal that you are worth ranking above an established competitor.
4. **Behavioural delay** — Google needs enough impressions and clicks to judge whether users are satisfied when they land on you.

None of these can be meaningfully rushed. What can be changed is how much progress you make inside each one.

## The Realistic Month-by-Month Timeline

This is what a competently run campaign looks like for a service business in a normal competitive market. Adjust it up for national or legal/finance markets, down for a small town with weak competitors.

| Phase | Timeframe | What Is Happening | What You Should See |
|---|---|---|---|
| Foundation | Month 1 | Technical audit, indexation fixes, Google Business Profile, tracking setup, keyword mapping | Crawl errors falling, pages getting indexed, baseline data in Search Console |
| Early signal | Months 2-3 | On-page optimisation, first content published, citations built | Impressions rising, long-tail keywords entering positions 20-50, first map pack movement |
| Traction | Months 4-6 | Content velocity, internal linking, link acquisition, review velocity | Page 1 for low-competition and local terms, organic leads beginning, AI citations appearing |
| Compounding | Months 7-12 | Scaling what ranks, topical depth, authority building | Page 1 for primary money terms, organic becoming a predictable lead channel |
| Defensible | Month 12+ | Maintenance, expansion, competitor displacement | Cost per lead falling as traffic grows without proportional spend |

The shape of that curve matters more than any single month. SEO does not improve linearly. It is flat, flat, flat, then it bends — because authority is cumulative and rankings are positional. Moving from position 18 to position 11 produces almost no traffic. Moving from position 4 to position 2 can double it.

### Why the curve bends so sharply

Click-through rate is brutally top-heavy. Recent 2026 benchmarks put position 1 at roughly 27.6% average organic CTR, position 2 near 15.6%, and position 3 around 10.4%. By position 8 you are into low single digits.

This is why month-five reports full of "we moved 40 keywords up an average of 6 positions" can be simultaneously true and worthless. Movement inside page two produces no leads. The work in months one through five is what makes the month-six-to-nine jump possible.

## What Actually Changes Your Timeline

Six months is a median, not a law. Five factors move it more than anything else.

**1. Your domain's existing authority.** A ten-year-old site with 200 referring domains ranks new content in weeks. A site registered last month competes against that. This is the single biggest variable, and it is the one agencies most often ignore when quoting timelines.

**2. Competition in your market.** "Personal injury lawyer Dallas" and "mobile welding Boerne" are both local searches. One is defended by firms spending six figures a year on SEO. The other may be defended by nobody. Same tactics, wildly different timelines.

**3. The state of your website.** If Google cannot crawl your site, nothing else matters. Sites built on JavaScript frameworks without server rendering, sites with accidental `noindex` tags, sites with three seconds of load time on mobile — these lose months before any content is written.

**4. Content velocity.** Publishing four solid pages a month beats publishing one. There is a floor below which you simply do not generate enough surface area to rank for anything.

**5. Whether you are also doing local SEO.** For service businesses, the map pack often ranks faster than organic listings — sometimes in four to eight weeks — because Google Business Profile signals are more directly controllable. If you have a physical address or defined service area, this is your fastest path. ## AI Search Changed the Timeline in Both Directions

Two things happened at once, and they pull opposite ways.

**The bad news: clicks got scarcer.** AI Overviews now trigger on roughly 48% of tracked queries. When an AI Overview appears, position 1 CTR falls from about 39.8% on a clean results page to around 19% — a drop of roughly 30%. Positions 2 through 5 have lost 33-38% of their clicks. Zero-click searches sat near 50% in 2019 and are around 65% in 2026, rising to about 83% on queries that trigger an AI Overview.

Ranking number one is worth less than it was three years ago. That is simply true, and any agency that tells you otherwise is selling you a 2019 product.

**The good news: citations arrive faster than rankings.** Getting cited inside an AI Overview, ChatGPT or Perplexity answer does not require the same domain authority that outranking an entrenched competitor does. It requires clearly structured, genuinely answer-shaped content with strong entity signals. We typically see first AI citations in four to eight weeks — well before page-one organic rankings arrive.

That is a meaningful shift in the timeline. The first six months used to be dead air. They no longer have to be. We cover the mechanics in [our guide to AI SEO](/blog/what-is-ai-seo).

## How to Tell It Is Working Before the Leads Arrive

This is the part that protects you. These are leading indicators — they move months before revenue does, and if they are flat at month three, something is wrong.

**Month 1-2, check:**
- Indexed page count rising in Google Search Console
- Crawl errors falling
- Google Business Profile views and direction requests trending up
- Core Web Vitals moving into "good"

**Month 2-4, check:**
- Total impressions in Search Console climbing, even if clicks are not
- Number of unique keywords generating impressions expanding
- Average position improving for your mapped target terms
- First AI Overview or ChatGPT citations appearing for brand and niche terms

**Month 4-6, check:**
- Clicks rising, not just impressions
- Keywords crossing from page 2 into page 1
- Map pack rankings for primary service + city terms
- First organic-attributed form fills and calls

**The single most useful early metric is impressions.** Impressions rising while clicks stay flat means Google is starting to show you but you are not yet ranked high enough to be clicked. That is exactly what healthy month-three SEO looks like. Impressions flat at month three means nothing is working, and you should be asking hard questions.

## The Honest Failure Cases

SEO does not work for everyone. It is worth knowing which side of the line you are on before you spend twelve months finding out.

SEO is a poor fit when:

- **You need leads this month to make payroll.** SEO cannot solve a cash-flow emergency. [Google Ads](/ads) can produce leads within days. Run ads for immediate flow and build SEO underneath it.
- **Nobody searches for what you sell.** Genuinely novel products have no search demand yet. You cannot optimise for a query that does not exist. That is a paid social and outbound problem.
- **Your margins cannot support a 6-12 month payback.** If your average customer is worth $300 and you close one in ten leads, the maths may never clear.
- **You will not commit past month four.** This is the most common failure by a wide margin. Businesses stop at month four — after paying for all of the cost and before receiving any of the benefit. That is the worst possible outcome, strictly worse than never starting.

If you are unsure whether the maths works for your business, start with what a customer is actually worth to you. Our breakdown of [how much a lead is worth](/blog/how-much-are-leads-worth) gives you the formula.

## The 90-Day Checklist

If you are starting SEO now, this is what should be finished in the first quarter. If your agency has not done these, the twelve-month timeline is already slipping.

**Days 1-30**
- Full technical audit: crawlability, indexation, redirects, canonicals
- Google Search Console and Analytics verified with conversion tracking live
- Google Business Profile fully completed — categories, services, service areas, photos
- Keyword research mapped to specific pages, with search intent labelled
- Baseline recorded: rankings, impressions, traffic, leads

**Days 31-60**
- Technical fixes shipped and verified in Search Console
- Core service pages rewritten and optimised
- Location pages built for each real service area
- First supporting content published
- Citation and directory cleanup underway

**Days 61-90**
- Content cadence established and holding
- Internal linking structure connecting supporting content to money pages
- Review generation running as a repeatable process
- Schema markup deployed, including FAQ markup for AI visibility
- First link acquisition landing

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can SEO work faster than three months?**
Sometimes. Local map pack rankings can move in four to eight weeks, and AI citations often appear in the same window. Technical fixes on a site with existing authority can produce jumps within weeks. Competitive organic rankings for high-value commercial terms rarely move that fast.

**Does paying more make SEO faster?**
Up to a point. More budget buys more content and more link acquisition, which compresses the timeline. It cannot compress crawl cycles or the time authority takes to accumulate. Doubling spend does not halve the timeline — expect meaningful compression, not miracles.

**How long until SEO pays for itself?**
Most businesses break even somewhere between month six and month fifteen, depending on customer value and market competition. After break-even the economics improve continuously, because traffic grows without proportional cost increases. This is the opposite of paid ads, where cost scales with volume.

**Will I lose rankings if I stop?**
Not immediately. Rankings decay rather than collapse — typically over three to six months as competitors publish and your content ages. SEO is closer to maintaining a building than paying rent, but it is not a one-time purchase.

**Is SEO still worth it with AI Overviews taking clicks?**
Yes, but the goal has shifted. Pure ranking-and-click-harvesting is a declining asset. Being the source that AI systems cite is a growing one. The work overlaps heavily — both reward clear, credible, well-structured content — but the measurement has to change.
