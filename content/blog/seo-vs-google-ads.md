**If you need leads this quarter, start with Google Ads. If you can fund six to twelve months of patience, SEO produces cheaper leads that keep arriving after you stop paying.** Most businesses that can afford both should run both — but almost nobody starts with both, and picking the wrong one first is an expensive mistake.

The honest framing is not "which is better." It is "which problem do you have right now?" One is a tap you can turn on this afternoon. The other is a well you have to dig.

## The Core Difference in One Paragraph

Google Ads is rented attention. You pay per click, you appear immediately, and the moment your card stops working you vanish from the page. SEO is owned attention. You invest in content, technical health and authority, you appear slowly, and the position persists after the spending slows. Rented is fast and repeatable. Owned is slow and compounding. Neither is a substitute for the other.

## The Trade-Off Table

| | Google Ads | SEO |
|---|---|---|
| Time to first lead | Days | 3-6 months for early signal, 6-12 for reliable flow |
| Cost per lead | Higher, and rises with competition | Lower, and falls as traffic compounds |
| What happens when you stop | Leads stop within days | Leads decay over 3-6 months |
| Budget behaviour | Scales with volume — more leads costs more | Roughly flat — more leads costs the same |
| Testing speed | Immediate. Query and message data within a week | Slow. Months per hypothesis |
| Position control | You can buy the top spot today | You earn it, or you do not |
| Best for | Urgent demand, new offers, seasonal peaks, testing | Established demand, long-term margin, defensibility |

One widely cited 2026 comparison puts SEO at roughly 8× ROI against Google Ads at roughly 4×, with cost per lead near $31 organic versus $181 paid. Treat those specific figures with caution — they are single-source, they average wildly different industries, and they measure a mature SEO programme against paid at any stage. The *direction* is right and well supported: organic leads cost less per lead once the programme matures. The magnitude depends entirely on your market.

## Start With Google Ads If...

**You need revenue inside 90 days.** SEO cannot solve a cash-flow problem. If missing this quarter's number is existential, ads are the only search channel that can respond in time.

**You are launching something new.** New location, new service line, new market. Ads tell you within two weeks whether demand exists at a price you can afford — a question SEO would take six months to answer.

**Your demand is seasonal or event-driven.** HVAC in the first heatwave, tax services in Q1, storm-response roofing. You need to be visible in a window, not in a year.

**You do not yet know what converts.** This is the underrated one. Ads generate search query data, landing page conversion data and message-testing data in weeks. That data makes the eventual SEO campaign dramatically better, because you build content around terms you have already proven convert — not terms a keyword tool guessed at.

**Your market is dominated by entrenched SEO competitors.** If the top ten organic results are national directories and firms fifteen years deep, buying position is realistic and earning it may not be.

## Start With SEO If...

**Your margins cannot absorb paid CPLs.** If a customer is worth $400 in gross profit and paid leads cost $180 with a 20% close rate, your cost per customer is $900. The maths never clears. Organic can, eventually.

**Your competitors are not doing it.** Plenty of local service markets still have weak organic competition — thin sites, unclaimed Google Business Profiles, no content. That is unusually cheap ground to take, and once taken it is hard to dislodge you.

**You are geographically defined.** Local SEO is the highest-ROI organic play for service businesses, and the map pack often moves in four to eight weeks — much faster than competitive organic rankings. If you have an address or a defined service area, you have a fast lane. Our [local SEO framework](/blog/local-seo-for-service-businesses) covers it.

**You are building an asset, not just a pipeline.** Organic rankings and AI citations carry into a business valuation in a way that an ad account does not.

**You are already spending heavily on ads and want the CPL down.** This is the most common trigger in practice. Businesses spending $15,000/month on Google Ads add SEO not to replace ads but to stop paid from being the only channel.

## The Answer for Most Businesses: Both, Sequenced

The genuine best practice is not choosing. It is sequencing, and letting each channel subsidise the other's weakness.

**Months 1-3 — Ads carry the load.**
Put most of the budget into Google Ads targeting your highest-intent, bottom-of-funnel terms. Simultaneously start SEO foundations: technical fixes, Google Business Profile, tracking, keyword mapping. Ads produce cash flow. SEO produces nothing visible yet, and that is expected.

**Months 4-6 — Ads inform SEO.**
You now have real search-term data showing which queries produce booked jobs, not just clicks. Build your SEO content plan around those proven terms. Meanwhile, early organic and map pack movement begins.

**Months 7-12 — Organic starts carrying weight.**
Organic leads begin arriving. Your blended cost per lead falls because a growing share of leads cost nothing incremental. Ad spend can hold steady while total lead volume rises.

**Month 12+ — Reallocate deliberately.**
With organic producing reliably, you have a real choice: cut paid spend and bank the margin, or hold spend and take more total market share. Most businesses that get here choose the second, because they can finally afford to.

### The budget split, roughly

If you have $3,000/month total: start near 70/30 paid/SEO, and drift toward 50/50 by month six.
If you have $10,000/month: 60/40 works from the start, because there is enough SEO budget for real content velocity immediately.
If you have under $1,500/month: pick one. Splitting it produces two underfunded campaigns and no leads. Which one depends on the cash-flow question above.

## What AI Search Changes About This Decision

This is the part most comparison articles have not caught up with.

AI Overviews now trigger on roughly 48% of tracked queries. When one appears, position 1 organic click-through falls from about 39.8% to around 19%. Zero-click searches have risen from about 50% in 2019 to roughly 65% in 2026, and around 83% on AI Overview queries.

Two consequences follow, and they cut in different directions:

**It weakens the pure-traffic case for SEO.** Ranking first is worth less than it used to be, because fewer people click anything at all. If your SEO pitch is "we will get you to number one and the traffic will follow," that pitch is depreciating.

**It strengthens the case for a specific kind of SEO.** Being the source an AI answer cites is now its own distribution channel, and it does not require the domain authority needed to outrank an incumbent. Citations often appear within four to eight weeks. That materially shortens the SEO payoff window — but only for work explicitly built for it: structured content, schema, entity signals, genuine expertise. See [our AI SEO guide](/blog/what-is-ai-seo).

Paid search is comparatively insulated so far — ads still sit above and around AI Overviews — but that is a rented insulation, and Google sets the rent.

## The Mistakes That Cost the Most

**Quitting SEO at month four.** You pay all of the cost and receive none of the benefit. Strictly worse than never starting. If you cannot commit to twelve months, put the money into ads instead.

**Running ads to a bad website.** Paid traffic amplifies whatever your site already does. If it converts at 1%, ads buy you expensive proof of that. Fix the landing experience first — our [landing page guide](/blog/what-is-a-landing-page) covers the anatomy — or you are buying clicks for a leaking bucket.

**Judging SEO on ad timelines.** Expecting month-two SEO to look like month-two ads guarantees you cancel a campaign that was working normally.

**Treating them as competitors internally.** They share keyword data, conversion data and landing pages. Run by separate teams that do not talk, you pay twice for the same learning.

**Bidding only on brand terms and calling it success.** Branded paid clicks convert beautifully because those people were already coming. Strip brand out before judging paid performance.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I just do Google Ads forever?**
Yes, and plenty of profitable businesses do. The risks are that your cost per lead rises as competitors bid up your market, you own nothing at the end, and one account suspension removes your entire pipeline. Ads-only is viable but fragile.

**Does ranking organically reduce my ad costs?**
Not directly — Quality Score does not read your organic position. Indirectly, yes: as organic supplies more leads, you can bid less aggressively for the same total volume, and you can stop bidding on terms you now rank for.

**What about Meta Ads instead of Google?**
Different intent entirely. Google captures existing demand; Meta creates it. For most service businesses fielding "I need this now" searches, Google comes first. Our [Google Ads vs Meta Ads comparison](/blog/google-ads-vs-meta-ads) covers when Meta is the better first move.

**How do I know if my SEO is working before leads arrive?**
Watch impressions and indexed pages in Search Console, map pack visibility, and first AI citations. Impressions rising while clicks stay flat is exactly what healthy month-three SEO looks like. More detail in [how long SEO takes](/blog/how-long-does-seo-take).

**Is SEO cheaper than ads?**
Per lead, at maturity, usually yes. In the first six months, no — you are paying full cost for close to zero output. The crossover typically falls between month six and month fifteen.
