Buy Exclusive Roofing Leads: Exclusive vs Shared, Costs & Best Sources

Buy exclusive roofing leads in 2026: exclusive vs shared, what they cost, where to get them, and how to avoid wasted lead spend.

Exclusive vs Shared Roofing Leads

Exclusive roofing leads are sold to only your company, so you're not racing competitors on response time, which is why they convert far better than shared marketplace leads. They cost more per lead but typically deliver lower cost per job. The best exclusive leads come from campaigns generated for you, not resold lists.

When a roofer searches "buy exclusive roofing leads," they are usually frustrated with shared leads. The pattern is familiar: you pay for a homeowner enquiry, you call within minutes, and three other roofers have already left voicemails. The race to the bottom on price and response speed has begun before you ever say hello. Exclusive leads remove that race entirely because the homeowner is connected to you and only you.

The difference shows up in your close rate. Shared leads spread the same prospect across multiple contractors, so even a strong sales process is fighting for a fraction of the deal. Exclusive leads let your reputation, pricing, and follow-up do the work without four competitors muddying the conversation. That is the whole reason exclusivity commands a premium, and the premium is usually worth it. For a deeper breakdown of the trade-offs, see our guide on exclusive vs shared leads.

What Exclusive Roofing Leads Cost

Pricing varies widely by market, storm activity, and lead source, so treat the following as 2026 ranges rather than fixed rates. In most US markets, exclusive residential roofing leads typically run $75 to $250 each, and they climb during storm season or in high-competition metros. Shared marketplace leads often sell for $20 to $80, which looks cheaper until you account for the lower close rate.

The number that actually matters is cost per booked job, not cost per lead. A $200 exclusive lead that closes one job in seven ($1,400 per job) is far cheaper per job than a $40 shared lead that closes one in fifty ($2,000 per job). Before you compare any source, calculate your own break-even using your average job value and close rate. Where to Buy Roofing Leads (and the catch)

Most roofers start with pay-per-lead marketplaces such as Angi, Networx, HomeAdvisor, or Modernize. They are fast to set up and require no marketing skill, which is the appeal. The catch is that the majority of these leads are shared by default, and the "exclusive" tier they upsell is often a thin slice of inventory at a steep markup. You also don't own the relationship or the data, so when you stop paying, the leads stop instantly.

The deeper catch is sourcing. When you buy a list or a marketplace lead, you cannot always tell how the lead was generated, how old it is, or whether the homeowner actually requested roofing help. That uncertainty is exactly why we wrote our guide on how to vet purchased leads. Position Xero does not sell lists. We generate leads through campaigns built for your company, so every lead is fresh, exclusive, and traceable to a specific ad or search.

Residential vs Commercial Roofing Leads

Residential and commercial roofing leads behave like two different businesses. Residential homeowners search Google, click Facebook ads, and respond to storm-driven urgency, so Google Ads for roofing companies and local SEO produce strong volume. The buying decision is often emotional and fast, especially after weather damage.

Commercial roofing is the opposite. The buyer is a property manager, facility manager, or general contractor who is not browsing consumer marketplaces. Commercial leads come from targeted search ads on commercial roof terms, direct outreach, LinkedIn, and referrals. The sales cycle is longer and the contract values are higher, which is why generic lead vendors rarely deliver usable commercial roofing leads. If commercial work is your goal, you need campaigns aimed at decision makers, not a bulk consumer list.
