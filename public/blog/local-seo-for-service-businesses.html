Local SEO for Service Businesses: How to Rank in Your City

The 5 pillars of local SEO for service businesses in 2026: Google Business Profile, reviews, citations, location pages, and AI SEO. A practical guide.

What Is Local SEO?

Local SEO is the process of optimising your business to appear in location-based search results on Google, including the local 3-pack (map results), Google Maps, and organic rankings for queries like "plumber near me" or "roofing company in Denver". It is the highest-ROI form of organic lead generation for service businesses with a defined geographic area.

Roughly 46% of Google searches carry local intent, and Google found 76% of people searching for something nearby on a phone visit a business within 24 hours. A local results page also holds two separate rankings: the map pack, and the organic links below it, scored by different systems. You can own the top organic spot and still be invisible in the pack that takes most of the clicks.

How Google Actually Ranks Local Businesses

Google names three factors: relevance, distance, and prominence.

  • Relevance is how well your profile and site match the query. You control it with categories, services, and page content.
  • Distance is how far you sit from the searcher. Mostly outside your control, and the reason rank shifts street by street: run a grid check and you are often number two outside your own shop and 17th four miles away.
  • Prominence is how well known you are: reviews, links, citations, and your organic rankings.

Pillar 1: Google Business Profile Optimisation

A fully built profile can rank you in the map pack months before your website ranks organically. Order of operations:

Categories

Primary category is the most important field on the profile. Pick the most specific one matching the service you want to sell: "roofing contractor" beats "contractor". Add up to nine secondaries, but only services you genuinely perform, since irrelevant ones dilute relevance and trigger suspension reviews. Calibrate against the primary category of the three profiles already ranking for your keyword.

Services and service areas

List every service separately with a 150 to 300 character description written the way you would explain it to a customer. Twenty-five described services give far more relevance surface than three. Service-area businesses should hide the address and set areas by city or zip, but stay realistic: listing 60 cities you cannot reach in an hour weakens the signal for the ones you can serve.

Products

Almost no service business uses Products, which is why it's worth 20 minutes. Package offers with a photo, fixed price, description, and a link: "Annual AC tune-up, $129". The tiles render inside the profile and give Google and AI answer engines another readable surface.

Photos

Twenty is the floor. Cover exterior, interior, team, vehicles, work in progress, and before-and-after pairs, then add three to five a month. A profile that stopped adding photos two years ago looks dormant to Google and to the person choosing who to call.

Posts

Post weekly, 100 to 300 words, one call to action. Standard posts expire after seven days, so cadence beats polish. They aren't a strong ranking lever, but they sustain the engagement that feeds prominence.

Q&A

You can ask and answer your own questions. Seed eight to twelve covering what people call to ask: price ranges, service boundaries, emergency availability, licensing, warranty, financing. Owner answers carry a verification marker and are frequently pulled into AI answers. Left empty, the section fills with strangers answering for you.

Pillar 2: Reviews and Review Velocity

Reviews move pack ranking, raise click-through from the pack, and close the sale before the phone rings. Treat them as a process with a monthly number attached.

What actually counts

Count matters relative to your local competitors, not in absolute terms. The practical rating sweet spot is 4.2 to 4.9, because a flawless 5.0 across eight reviews converts worse than a 4.7 across 180. Recency gets ignored most: 200 reviews with the newest 14 months old loses to 60 reviews with four from this month. Keywords inside review text count too, so ask customers to name the job you did.

The velocity maths

A competitor at 120 reviews adding five a month needs roughly ten a month from you to be passed inside a year. Close 40 jobs a month, ask all 40, and at a typical 25% response rate you get your ten. That's the entire system.

How to ask, and how to respond

Ask at job completion, before invoice friction: verbally first, then a text within two hours with your direct review link. SMS typically outperforms email three to five times here. Three touches is enough. Never screen for happy customers first, because review gating violates Google policy and gets review sets removed in bulk. Respond to everything within 48 hours, mentioning the service and city once on positives, and staying under four sentences on negatives. Do not buy reviews: Google removes purchased sets in batches, and the FTC's 2024 rule made buying or selling them a civil-penalty offence in the US.

Pillar 3: Citations and NAP Consistency

A citation is any mention of your business name, address, and phone number on another site: a directory listing with defined fields, or a mention inside page copy like a league sponsorship. They carry less weight than a decade ago but still work as verification, since Google cross-references your details to decide how confident it is that you exist where you say you do. Fifty accurate listings beat five hundred bought ones.

The core set worth building by hand

Apple Business Connect, Bing Places, Yelp, Facebook, Nextdoor, BBB, Angi, Thumbtack, Yellow Pages, Foursquare, your Chamber of Commerce, and three to six industry directories such as Houzz for remodellers, Avvo for law firms, or Healthgrades for medical practices. Then push the aggregators, Data Axle and Localeze, which feed hundreds of smaller sites. One afternoon, done.

What inconsistency costs you

Google matches entities, not strings, so "Suite 200" versus "Ste. 200" is harmless. Conflicting data is not. The classic: you moved three years ago and 40 directories still carry the old address, so Google sees two competing locations and trusts both less. Second: call-tracking numbers scattered across directories, so five phone numbers claim to be you. Fix duplicate Google profiles first, since a duplicate splits reviews and can suppress both listings, then old addresses, phone mismatches, then name variants.

Pillar 4: Location Pages That Are Not Doorway Pages

The standard failure mode is 40 pages of the same 600 words with the city swapped. Google's spam policies name doorway pages explicitly, and the usual result is URLs sitting in Search Console as "Crawled, currently not indexed" while dragging quality signals across the rest of the site.

The test before building one: what would someone in this city need to know that someone in the next city would not? If you can't answer, don't build it. What each page genuinely needs:

  • The named crew, tech, or branch covering that area, with a real photo
  • Photos from jobs actually completed in that city
  • Review text from customers in that city, pulled from real reviews
  • Conditions specific to the area: hard water, clay soil movement, hail season, salt corrosion
  • Permit reality, including which office issues it and typical turnaround
  • Coverage detail: "the 44 zip codes between X and Y, typical arrival 35 to 50 minutes"
  • Pricing that reflects that market, if your pricing genuinely varies
  • Neighbourhoods and landmarks inside real sentences, never as a footer keyword dump
  • A unique title, meta description, H1, and LocalBusiness or Service schema
  • An internal link from a real navigation path, not an orphan only the sitemap knows

Five strong location pages beat forty thin ones. Start with the three to five cities where you already have job density and reviews, then add one at a time. Pillar 5: AI SEO for Local Queries

AI Overviews now appear for a large share of local service queries, and more people ask ChatGPT or Perplexity for a recommendation before opening Maps. Those answers are assembled from four sources:

  1. Your Google Business Profile. Hours, services, ratings, and category are read straight off it, so profile completeness is AI SEO whether you call it that or not.
  2. Your reviews. Models summarise sentiment, so your customers' words become the description of your business. If eight reviews say "showed up same day", that phrase ends up in the answer.
  3. Third-party lists. "Best plumbers in Tulsa" roundups are frequently the cited source, so you often enter an AI answer through someone else's page first.
  4. Your own pages, but only when a clean, extractable answer sits within two or three sentences of a heading.

Practically: FAQPage and LocalBusiness schema with an accurate areaServed, one question per heading phrased the way people ask it, a 40 to 60 word answer underneath, and published price ranges, since models favour pages that state numbers over pages that say "contact us for a quote". You cannot rank-track AI answers. Substitute a monthly manual test of 15 to 20 core queries across ChatGPT, Google AI Mode, and Perplexity, logging where you appear and what got cited.

The 90-Day Local SEO Checklist

In order. Do not skip ahead to links.

  1. Claim and verify your profile, and remove duplicate listings.
  2. Set the most specific primary category, then add only relevant secondaries.
  3. Describe every service individually and set realistic service areas.
  4. Upload 20+ real photos and write the full 750-character description.
  5. Add packaged offers as products, with prices and links.
  6. Seed 8 to 12 owner-answered questions in Q&A.
  7. Build the core 12 to 15 citations by hand and push the aggregators.
  8. Fix NAP conflicts: duplicates, old addresses, phone mismatches, name variants.
  9. Launch the review request sequence with a monthly target.
  10. Add LocalBusiness and FAQPage schema sitewide.
  11. Rewrite your top three service pages with direct answers and price ranges.
  12. Build three to five location pages with genuinely local proof.
  13. Start weekly posts and a three-to-five photo monthly cadence.
  14. Earn 5 to 10 local links: chamber, suppliers, sponsorships, local press.
  15. Run a grid rank check monthly and answer every review within 48 hours, permanently.

Expect profile work to move rankings in weeks, reviews and on-page work to compound through months two and three, and links to be what separates you from the other well-optimised business in town by month six.
