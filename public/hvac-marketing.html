HVAC Marketing

An HVAC Marketing Agency Measured on Booked Jobs
Not Leads in a Spreadsheet.

Built for the HVAC demand calendar, not a generic home-services template.

Emergency repair, replacement, and maintenance plans are three different businesses on one truck. We build them as three campaigns with three landing pages and three cost-per-booked-job targets. Google Ads management from $850/month. Last updated: August 2026.

What is HVAC marketing? HVAC marketing is the mix of paid search, Local Services Ads, local SEO, and website work an HVAC contractor uses to generate service calls, replacement quotes, and maintenance-plan signups. A specialist HVAC marketing agency optimises toward booked jobs on the dispatch board rather than form fills, because an HVAC lead that never gets on the schedule is worth nothing.

The Difference

Why HVAC Marketing Is Not Generic Home-Services Marketing

Most agency pages swap the word "roofing" for "HVAC" and call it a vertical. Four things actually change.

Demand is weather-triggered. An HVAC phone rings on the first 100-degree afternoon and again on the first hard freeze. Searches for "AC not cooling" can multiply inside 48 hours, and click prices climb with them as every competitor's bidding chases the same spike.

Three job types with different economics share one ad account. A $250 capacitor swap, a $9,000 changeout, and a $199 maintenance plan cannot share a budget, a bid strategy, or a landing page. Blend them and you lose sight of which one funds the business.

The buying window on an emergency is minutes. A homeowner with no cooling is not comparing proposals. They are calling companies in the order Google listed them and booking the first one that answers with a same-day slot.

Capacity is a hard ceiling. Overspend in peak and you generate calls you cannot service, and unserviced calls become one-star reviews that raise your acquisition cost for a year. Real HVAC lead generation throttles to truck capacity on purpose.

Seasonality

The HVAC Demand Calendar and How Budget Should Flex Across It

An equal monthly ad budget is the clearest sign an agency has never run an HVAC account. Demand is not flat, so spend should not be.

Period What is selling Budget posture Campaign priority
Feb – Apr Pre-season tune-ups, maintenance plans 60–70% of peak Plan and tune-up offers, SEO build
May – Sep Emergency repair, emergency replacement Well above baseline Emergency search and Local Services Ads
Sep – Oct Heating tune-ups, planned replacement 60–70% of peak Financing-led replacement, plan renewals
Nov – Jan No-heat calls, freeze-event emergencies Baseline plus held reserve Heating emergency, weather-triggered

Plan the year, then spend the week

Set the annual media number first and allocate it by expected demand, not by calendar month. Then manage inside the week: push bids up on forecast heat waves and cold snaps, and pull back on mild days when the same click converts at half the rate.

Shoulder season buys next summer's margin

Maintenance-plan members are the cheapest replacement leads you will ever have. You own the relationship, the equipment history, and the technician standing in the mechanical room when the system dies. Every plan sold in March is a near-free replacement opportunity in July, which is why shoulder budget should move into plan signups, tune-up offers, and SEO groundwork rather than disappear.

Peak season is a bidding problem, not a volume problem

In cooling season you do not need more demand, you need to win existing demand without overpaying: tight geographic bidding by drive time, hard negatives on DIY and parts searches, and call-first formats during dispatch hours. Our breakdown of how much Google Ads cost covers the mechanics.

Lead Economics

Emergency, Replacement and Maintenance Plans Need Different Campaigns

Same trucks, three different buyers. Each needs its own campaign, its own landing page, and its own definition of a booked job.

Emergency Repair

Maximum intent, zero patience. Highest cost per click in the account, fastest payback. The page is a phone number, a service-area confirmation, and the next available slot. A form is the fallback, not the primary call to action.

Replacement & Install

A five-figure decision. Buyers collect two or three quotes, compare financing, and take days. The page needs financing terms, equipment options, rebate details, and estimate booking. A much higher cost per booked job here is correct, not a failure.

Maintenance Plans

Low ticket, compounding value. Rarely worth peak-season click prices. Won through shoulder-season offers, remarketing to past site visitors, and email to your existing list, then they feed replacement revenue for years.

The most common waste in HVAC accounts is all three pointed at one "Contact Us" page. When "AC repair open now" and "new AC unit cost" land on the same page, both convert worse than they should and neither result is diagnosable. Separate intent, separate page. That is what conversion-focused landing page design is for.

The Real Number

What a Booked HVAC Job Should Actually Cost

Cost per lead is a vanity metric in this trade. Cost per booked job decides whether the marketing pays for itself.

Across competitive US metros, industry benchmarks put a booked service or emergency repair call somewhere around $75 to $250, and a booked in-home replacement estimate closer to $300 to $1,200. Those are market ranges, not promises, and they move with metro competitiveness and season.

The arithmetic is simple enough to run yourself. On emergency terms, peak-season clicks commonly land between roughly $8 and $25. A purpose-built emergency page converting at 10% turns a $15 click into a $150 lead. If your office books 60% of those calls onto the schedule, cost per booked job is $250. Set that against your average repair ticket and your replacement rate off a service call, and you will know in four minutes whether the channel works.

Why a cheap lead is often the expensive option

A $40 lead that books at 20% costs $200 per job. A $120 lead that books at 70% costs $171. The cheaper lead is the worse buy, and an agency reporting only cost per lead shows you the wrong winner every time. For wider context, see our cost per lead benchmarks by industry.

Attribution has to follow the job, not the click

Booked-job reporting needs tracking numbers at campaign level, a disposition field in the dispatch software, and that outcome pushed back into the ad platform. Without the loop, automated bidding optimises toward whichever calls are easiest to generate, usually the ones your team cannot sell.

Speed to Lead

Speed to Lead Decides Who Wins the Emergency Call

The most valuable improvement available to most HVAC contractors is not a better ad. It is answering the phone.

Widely cited lead-response research finds that contacting an inbound lead within five minutes makes it far more likely to qualify than waiting thirty, and that most buyers go with whoever responds first. In HVAC the effect is sharper, because the alternative to your company is not "think about it later." It is the next number on the results page, called ninety seconds after yours rang out.

Practically: tracked numbers per campaign so you can see which ads produce ringing phones, call-first formats during dispatch hours, missed-call-to-text so an unanswered ring becomes a booked slot instead of a competitor's job, and after-hours routing to a service that can schedule rather than take a message. None of it is glamorous. All of it moves cost per booked job further than a headline rewrite.

The sequence that works for most contractors: switch on Local Services Ads first because they sit above the paid results and book fastest, run Google Search campaigns alongside them for replacement demand where message control matters, and start local SEO immediately even though it will not pay for months. Market Focus

Dallas-Fort Worth: A Multi-Peril HVAC Market

DFW is not a bigger version of a normal HVAC market. It has a different demand shape, and the budget should reflect it.

The metroplex holds roughly 8.48 million people and posted the second-highest absolute population gain of any US metro in 2024–25, adding over 123,000 residents. That is about 11% growth since 2020, faster than any other top-five metro. New business formation is up 19.7% over five years, Texas led the nation in business growth at 14% year over year, and 82% of Dallas businesses have fewer than twenty employees.

It is also a multi-peril market. DFW sits in Hail Alley, taking three to six damaging hail events a year plus spring tornado activity. Summers are long and punishing. Winter freeze events produce no-heat surges and burst-pipe emergencies. Unlike a single-season metro such as Phoenix, a Dallas contractor faces a spring storm bump, a long summer peak, and a winter freeze spike. You budget for three demand events and hold a real reserve for the freeze, because it arrives with no notice and clears in a week.

Geography matters more here than almost anywhere. Bidding identically on Frisco, Plano, Arlington, Fort Worth, Irving, McKinney and Denton ignores drive time, and drive time is margin. Segmenting service areas by travel radius rather than city name is one of the fastest cost-per-booked-job wins in a large-metro account.

Buyer's Guide

Questions to Ask Before Hiring an HVAC Marketing Agency

We are a new agency with no client roster to parade, and we are not going to invent one. Here is something more useful: the questions that separate a real HVAC operator from a rebranded generalist. Ask us the same ones.

01

What is my cost per booked job?

If the answer describes form fills or "leads," the loop back to your dispatch board does not exist. Ask how a booked job is recorded and where that data goes.

02

How does my budget change from March to July?

A flat monthly number all year means nobody has thought about the demand calendar. Ask for the shape of the annual plan before you sign.

03

How many landing pages will I have?

One page for emergency, replacement and maintenance plans is a red flag. Different intent needs different pages, offers, and calls to action.

04

What happens to a lead at 9pm Saturday?

Ask exactly how after-hours calls and forms are handled, and whether missed-call-to-text is in place. Peak-season emergencies ignore office hours.

05

Who owns the assets if I leave?

The ad account, tracking numbers, landing pages and historical data should be yours. If they are not, you are renting your own marketing history.
